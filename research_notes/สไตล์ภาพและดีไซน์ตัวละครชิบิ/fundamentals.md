# Chibi / Super-Deformed (SD) Character Design Fundamentals

## Origin and history of chibi/SD

### Takeaway
"Chibi" comes from Japanese *chibi kyara* ("tiny character"). "Super deformed" comes from *deforume* (stylistic distortion, from the French *déformer*). The style was popularized by Bandai's SD Gundam capsule toys (1985) and later by figure lines such as Nendoroid (2006).

### Cited Findings
- English "chibi" comes from *chibi kyara* (ちびキャラ, "tiny character"). In Japanese, *chibi* is a colloquial word for very short people or children, from *chibiru* (禿びる, "to wear down"). — [Wikipedia: Chibi (style)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chibi_(style))
- "Super deformed"/"SD" comes from Japanese *deforume* (デフォルメ, stylistic distortion), from the French *déformer*. The two terms are often used interchangeably, but "chibi" can mean any small character, while SD refers specifically to exaggerated proportions. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chibi_(style))
- Definition: small, chubby characters with stubby limbs, oversized heads and eyes, tiny noses and bodies, and minimal detail. The style is part of kawaii culture and is used in advertising. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chibi_(style))
- Early examples: Gen Sato's 4-panel gag manga *Ganbare Alfin-chan* (chibi *Crusher Joe* cast) ran in *Animage* in Oct 1982 and in OUT in 1983. Kaiyodo's Alfin-chan figure is called the first chibi figure. OUT also ran chibi *Yamato* parodies. — [Tofugu](https://tofugu.com/japanese/chibi)
- SD Gundam: student Koji Yokoi sent chibi Gundam fan art to Bandai's magazine *Model News* (Mokei Jouhou). In 1984 Bandai's toy department invited him to design capsule toys, and the *SD Gundam World* gashapon launched in 1985. Some Bandai staff resisted because it clashed with the franchise's "real robot" image. Yokoi drew on the mini-Godzilla and mini-Ultraman in Toriyama's *Dr. Slump*. — [Tofugu](https://tofugu.com/japanese/chibi); [Wikipedia: SD Gundam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SD_Gundam)
- "Super Deformed" became standard Japanese usage through SD Gundam, and the terms "chibi kyara"/"mini kyara" followed soon after. — [Tofugu](https://tofugu.com/japanese/chibi)
- Bandai has sold 176.6M SD Gundam model units since 1987 (as of March 2021). — [Wikipedia: SD Gundam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SD_Gundam)
- Nendoroid: Good Smile Company, since 2006. Created by Tsuyoshi Oda. The first figure was Neco Arc (Wonder Festival 2006). Figures are about 10 cm tall with a large head and small body, and have swappable face plates (over 5,000 exist). — [Wikipedia: Nendoroid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nendoroid)
- Typical use in anime/manga is childlike versions of popular characters and comedic or gag scenes. — [Tofugu](https://tofugu.com/japanese/chibi). Western shows use it for exaggerated emotional reactions: *Teen Titans*, *Avatar*'s "Super Deformed Shorts", *RWBY*. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chibi_(style))

### Inferences
- In anime, chibi traditionally marks a tonal shift (gag, cute aside). A series drawn entirely in chibi reads as light or cozy by default, so serious beats need deliberate expression and lighting.

### Gaps
- No primary Bandai or Japanese-language source was checked for the Yokoi account. Tofugu and fan wikis are secondary sources.

## Head-to-body proportions and variants

### Takeaway
Chibi characters are usually 2–3 heads tall (sometimes 1.5 or 4), against 7–8 heads for a normal anime figure. Fewer heads reads as more babyish and cuter, more heads reads as more mature. Torso and leg ratio further tunes the effect.

### Cited Findings
- A normal anime character is about 7–8 heads tall. In SD, the head is typically 1/3 to 1/2 of total height. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chibi_(style))
- 2–3 heads is usual. Head-heavy designs are very cute and babyish and "might tip over". Body-heavy designs look like a manga 4-year-old, and the long body pulls focus from the head. Balanced is the "safe zone". — [CSP Tips 4863](https://tips.clip-studio.com/en-us/articles/4863)
- 2-head and 3-head proportions are recommended. A 1.5-head figure works best with simplified clothing. Body types range from square/cylindrical (no neck, cutest) through balanced and rounded (emphasis on legs) to slender (near-realistic, less cute). Small-size tricks (no neck, exaggerated thighs or hips, pointed feet) should not be stacked on 3-head designs. — [CSP Tips 4868](https://tips.clip-studio.com/en-us/articles/4868)
- 2–4 heads tall, with a 1:1 torso-to-leg ratio as balanced. A longer torso looks cuter and more doll-like, and longer legs look more mature. No ratio is mandatory. — [Clip Studio official guide](https://www.clipstudio.net/how-to-draw/archives/155423)
- Nendoroid is the 3D commercial variant, with a large head, small body, and swappable faces. — [Wikipedia: Nendoroid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nendoroid)

### Inferences
- For a narrator mascot that must emote at phone size, 2–2.5 heads maximizes face area. Use 3 heads when the body must act (holding objects, walking).

### Gaps
- I found no authoritative taxonomy of the names "mini chibi" or "deformed" tiers. These are informal artist terms.

## Anatomy simplification: eyes, face, hands/feet, shapes, expressions, poses

### Takeaway
The general rule is to exaggerate the head, eyes, and hair, deform the hands, feet, and torso, and omit the nose and fingers. Build from round shapes, place features low and close together, and let the eyes and mouth carry emotion.

### Cited Findings
- Exaggerate the head, eyes, and hair. Deform the feet, hands, and torso. Omit the nose and fingers. — [CSP Tips 4863](https://tips.clip-studio.com/en-us/articles/4863)
- Torso: bean or pear shape with weight low "like a water-filled balloon", no real shoulders, curved hips. The neck is often omitted. Hair is drawn in lumps, not strands. — [CSP 4863](https://tips.clip-studio.com/en-us/articles/4863)
- Eyes sit middle-to-low on the skull and not too far apart. The nose is a dot or shadow, or absent. The mouth is small by default. Shocked or angry: very low, touching the face outline. Upbeat or talkative: big. Shy or sad: small. Hands can lose fingers, or rarely become a circle. — [CSP 4863](https://tips.clip-studio.com/en-us/articles/4863)
- Head shapes: anime pointed chin, fully round, or round cheeks with a flat chin (cutest). Dot eyes suit simple styles, and anime eyes give more range. With no nose, place the mouth slightly higher. Eyebrows can be omitted, with emotion shown through eye shape. Plump "sausage" hands suit 1.5–2-head figures. Use thicker lines for simpler styles. — [CSP 4868](https://tips.clip-studio.com/en-us/articles/4868)
- Features move lower and closer together. The nose, mouth, and chin shrink. Hands and feet become stubby or simple circles and triangles. Limb shape should match the body (chubby limbs with a bean body). — [CSP 4897](https://tips.clip-studio.com/en-us/articles/4897)
- Eye line slightly below center. Smooth curves instead of jawlines. Sloping shoulders. Thick limbs, because slim limbs look too mature. Hands are a thumb plus a four-finger mitten on 2-head figures, with more detail at 3 heads. Eyes carry most of the emotion (up/down-turned, round/thin). Straight-line eyes suit comedy, and expressions can go extreme. Poses can ignore gravity and the skeleton, with cuteness over realism. — [Clip Studio official guide](https://www.clipstudio.net/how-to-draw/archives/155423)
- Silhouette check: fill the figure solid black and see whether it still reads. — [Walt Disney Family Museum PDF](https://www.waltdisney.org/sites/default/files/2020-05/T%26T_Silhouette-final2.pdf); [Creative Bloq](https://www.creativebloq.com/art/character-design/how-to-design-instantly-recognisable-characters-using-strong-silhouettes)

### Inferences
- Emotional range in chibi comes from a small vocabulary of eye and mouth shapes plus exaggerated body language, much like Nendoroid face plates. A fixed expression sheet helps keep consistency across AI-generated frames.

### Gaps
- I found no rigorous source on pose libraries. The guidance is limited to "gravity optional" and "balance body-heavy designs with poses".

## Common mistakes and best practices

### Takeaway
Most mistakes come from leaving too much realism or adult anatomy in.

### Cited Findings
- Avoid breasts or abs (they make the chibi look adult), hook noses, overly long bodies without a head focus, broad shoulders, and individual hair strands. — [CSP 4863](https://tips.clip-studio.com/en-us/articles/4863)
- Avoid stacking small-size tricks on 3-head designs, putting 1.5 heads with detailed clothing, over-defining the nose or lips, and picking an iris color off the original. — [CSP 4868](https://tips.clip-studio.com/en-us/articles/4868)
- A too-realistic face or body on chibi proportions looks uncanny. Don't mix realistic limbs with a stylized body. — [CSP 4897](https://tips.clip-studio.com/en-us/articles/4897)
- Avoid sharp jawlines, detailed fingers on 2-head figures, and slender limbs. "Reduce information while leaving the key features." — [Clip Studio official guide](https://www.clipstudio.net/how-to-draw/archives/155423)

### Inferences
- For AI image prompts, state negatives explicitly: no realistic anatomy, no detailed fingers, no sharp jaw, and a consistent head ratio.

## Keeping a character recognizable when converting to chibi

### Takeaway
Chibi bodies are nearly identical across characters, so identity has to come from exaggerated signature traits: hair silhouette, eyebrows, accessories, glasses, clothing shapes, and exact color, especially eye color.

### Cited Findings
- Because chibi body shapes are identical regardless of age, gender, or height, exaggerate each character's distinguishing features, such as glasses size, hair length, and clothing. Hair that resists gravity or has a unique silhouette distinguishes characters. — [Clip Studio official guide](https://www.clipstudio.net/how-to-draw/archives/155423)
- "Look for big shapes in the character's design and key features that make the character unique." Skip fine detail. — [CSP 4897](https://tips.clip-studio.com/en-us/articles/4897)
- Keep signature traits, such as large eyebrows staying large, and simplify the rest. Keep the iris color close to the original. — [CSP 4868](https://tips.clip-studio.com/en-us/articles/4868)
- Emphasizing signature traits such as unusual hair or accessories is a defining feature of SD. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chibi_(style))
- Test identity with a solid-black silhouette. — [Creative Bloq](https://www.creativebloq.com/art/character-design/how-to-design-instantly-recognisable-characters-using-strong-silhouettes)

### Inferences
- Workflow: list 3–5 identity markers (hair shape, a key accessory, a palette with hex values, eye color, an outfit icon). Exaggerate them, drop everything else, then run a silhouette test and keep a reference sheet attached to every generation.

### Gaps
- No published design book (for example, Japanese SD how-to books) was consulted directly.
