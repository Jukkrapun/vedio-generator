# Semi-Realistic Anime Art Style and Character Design Fundamentals

## Definition: where the style sits, and related labels

### Takeaway
Semi-realistic anime keeps anime's graphic simplification (large but grounded eyes, clean shapes, controlled shading) and pulls proportions, facial structure and lighting toward real anatomy. There is no formal definition. Several neighboring traditions get grouped under the label: Shinkai films (simple characters over photo-based backgrounds), Korean manhwa realism, Chinese gacha cel shading, and painterly 2D/3D hybrids such as Arcane.

### Cited Findings
- The core difficulty is "blending realistic features with an anime look", and there is no single method for doing it. — [Animeoutline: Realistic Anime Face](https://animeoutline.com/?p=5477)
- The more stylized a drawing is, the less realistic its features should be, and the features should match each other in level of stylization. — [Tutsplus: How to Draw Anime Heads and Faces](https://design.tutsplus.com/tutorials/how-to-draw-anime-heads-and-faces--cms-31884)
- **Shinkai look:** character art is fairly simple and mainstream TV-anime style (designer Masayoshi Tanaka: Toradora, AnoHana, Your Name, Weathering With You). The visual weight comes from photo-referenced, "hyper-realistic yet surreally soft" backgrounds. The realism is in the environment and light more than in the faces. — [Cartoon Brew retrospective](https://cartoonbrew.com/anime/makoto-shinkai-career-retrospective-your-name-225859.html); [TV Tropes: Makoto Shinkai](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Creator/MakotoShinkai); [GKIDS: Character Designs of Weathering With You](https://gkids.com/2020/08/05/the-character-designs-of-weathering-with-you/)
- An informal style guide warns that pushing Shinkai-style characters too realistic makes the frame "look like a CG render" and lose the Shinkai feel. This is fan commentary, not authoritative. — [Anifusion style page](https://anifusion.ai/style/makoto-shinkai-style)
- **Korean manhwa realism:** Japanese comics lean on "hyperstylistics and exaggerations", while manhwa "has always been more realistic and grounded". Solo Leveling (art by Jang Sung-rak / DUBU, Redice Studio) is described as having "sharp manhwa style... with a realistic lean". Webtoons are built for vertical scrolling. — [Arkhaven: Toon-Up Solo Leveling](https://arkhaven.substack.com/p/toon-up-solo-leveling); [ComicBook Yeti review](https://www.comicbookyeti.com/post/solo-leveling-webtoon-manhwa-series)
- **Chinese gacha style (Genshin Impact / miHoYo):** cel-shaded 3D characters with faked, art-directed shading and hand-painted shadows, made to look 2D. miHoYo chose anime-style "art core pillars" and reportedly avoided ray tracing to protect the look. — [GDC Vault: Genshin Impact, Crafting an Anime Style Open World](https://gdcvault.com/play/1027538/-Genshin-Impact-Crafting-an); [B. J. Ayers: Recreating the Genshin shader](https://bjayers.artstation.com/blog/category/5); [TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/genshin-impact-art-style-ray-tracing/)
- **Arcane (Fortiche):** combines drawing, 2D painting and 3D animation. Shadows and highlights are hand-painted onto 3D models, backgrounds are matte paintings projected onto simple 3D geometry, and FX such as smoke, fire and muzzle flashes are hand-drawn 2D layered over the 3D. Lines are freehand and slightly shaky, and the stated goal was to carry the rough, painterly quality of the concept art into animation. — [RedShark News](https://redsharknews.com/why-netflixs-arcane-looks-so-good-how-fortiche-ramped-up-the-animation-pipeline); [80.lv: Arcane backgrounds](https://80.lv/articles/arcane-artists-show-how-they-combine-traditional-art-3d-for-backgrounds); [VFX Voice: Arcane S2](https://vfxvoice.com/riot-games-and-fortiche-get-revolutionary-with-arcane-season-2/)
- **Ilya Kuvshinov:** a secondary source describes his style as a fusion of anime and realism. He trained academically, and his teachers called the anime-leaning style "not very academic". He did art direction and character work in Japanese animation. — [Journal du Japon interview, 2020](https://dev.journaldujapon.com/2020/09/09/meeting-a-russian-artist-in-japanese-animation-and-more-ilya-kuvshinov/); [ArtStation Magazine 2014](https://magazine.artstation.com/2014/07/concept-artist-art-lead-ilya-kuvshinov/)

### Inferences
- The style is a spectrum rather than one look, because different works put the realism in different places. Shinkai puts it in the environment and light, manhwa in anatomy and faces, Arcane in painterly texture and lighting, and Genshin in 3D form while keeping 2D-looking shading.
- For a Thai 2 a.m. channel, the Shinkai approach (simple, appealing faces with realistic night light and environments) is the lowest-risk version, because it avoids realism in the face, where the uncanny valley is most likely.

### Gaps
- I found no authoritative or academic definition of "semi-realism" or "anime realism". These are community terms.
- Spider-Verse was not researched directly, so no sourced detail is included for it.
- I found no sourced comparative analysis of Wit Studio or MAPPA realism (for example Attack on Titan or Jujutsu Kaisen designs).

## Proportions and anatomy; face construction

### Takeaway
Bodies run about 7 to 8 heads tall, close to realistic and taller than standard anime. Faces stay anime-readable: eyes larger than real but narrower vertically than typical anime eyes, a real nose built from a few curves with the bridge drawn on one side only, optional lips, a flatter and wider chin, and shading kept in a few planned shadow zones. Hair is drawn as clumps with open, strand-split ends.

### Cited Findings
- Realistic and semi-realistic bodies are typically 7 to 8 heads tall. Anime uses fewer heads because the head is bigger (no exact number given). Chibi is 2 to 4 heads tall. Keep basic anatomy even when proportions are exaggerated. — [Clip Studio Tips: article 8014](https://tips.clip-studio.com/en-us/articles/8014)
- Chin: flatter and wider than a typical anime chin, closer to a real one. The head can be slightly taller (optional). — [Animeoutline](https://animeoutline.com/?p=5477)
- Eyes: placed below the horizontal midline, about one eye-width apart. Narrower vertically than typical anime eyes but larger than real eyes. Irises stay round rather than vertically stretched. — [Animeoutline](https://animeoutline.com/?p=5477)
- Nose: a semi-realistic face uses a nose shaped closer to a real one instead of the anime dot. Build it from two small nostril curves, two side curves, one curve for the tip and one long curve for the bridge. Draw the bridge on one side only, because both sides looks odd. — [Animeoutline](https://animeoutline.com/?p=5477)
- Lips: anime mouths often have no lips, but lips can be added for realism. Place the bottom lip halfway between the nose and the chin, drawn as a wide curve with a slight dip in the middle. Add a small white highlight on the lip. — [Animeoutline](https://animeoutline.com/?p=5477)
- Skin shading: shade only the likely shadow zones, which are under the forehead hair, around the eyelids, under the nose, the upper neck and the inner hair. — [Animeoutline](https://animeoutline.com/?p=5477)
- Hair: draw it in clumps with the ends left open rather than pointed, and add strand lines into the openings. Plan front, side and back/top masses, then add smaller splits. Leave highlights white. — [Animeoutline](https://animeoutline.com/?p=5477)
- In commercial semi-real 3D work (Genshin), the shading is faked and art-directed instead of physically computed, which keeps it readable as anime. — [B. J. Ayers](https://bjayers.artstation.com/blog/category/5)
- Loish's stylized anatomy: she studies shape and gesture rather than precise detail, alternating work from memory and from reference so the result stays between realistic and cartoony. She moved from realistic rendering toward stylized anatomy "for authenticity over copying photos". — [Creative Bloq: Loish character design](https://www.creativebloq.com/how-to/character-design-by-loish); [Loish Patreon](https://www.patreon.com/posts/how-i-developed-93390904)

### Inferences
- Practical recipe: realistic skull and jaw, realistic body proportions of about 7 to 7.5 heads, then exaggerate only the eyes (about 1.2 to 1.5 times real size; this ratio is my estimate, unsourced) and simplify the line count around the mouth and nose.
- Lighting realism (rim light, soft bounce, environment color) adds a "realistic" feel more safely than adding facial detail such as pores or wrinkles.

### Gaps
- I found no sourced numeric eye-to-face ratio for semi-realistic anime.
- The requested 6.5 to 7.5 heads range is not confirmed. The only source found says 7 to 8.

## Notable artists, studios and works: how each balances stylization

### Takeaway
Each reference point sets the stylization dial differently, and none of them makes everything realistic at once.

### Cited Findings
- Shinkai / Tanaka: simple mainstream anime characters against photo-based, detailed backgrounds, with mood prioritized over intricate character work. — [Cartoon Brew](https://cartoonbrew.com/anime/makoto-shinkai-career-retrospective-your-name-225859.html); [Sakugabooru blog: Shinkai context](https://blog.sakugabooru.com/2021/12/15/shinkai-context/)
- Kuvshinov: anime-leaning faces painted over academic training; the description is secondary. — [Journal du Japon](https://dev.journaldujapon.com/2020/09/09/meeting-a-russian-artist-in-japanese-animation-and-more-ilya-kuvshinov/)
- Loish: stylized, gesture-driven shapes with expressive, adjusted color. She uses Selective Color and limited palettes for emotion. — [Creative Bloq](https://www.creativebloq.com/how-to/character-design-by-loish); [Proko livestream](https://www.proko.com/lesson/stylization-exercise-with-loish-livestream)
- Solo Leveling: a realistic lean, detailed and dynamic action art. — [ComicBook Yeti](https://www.comicbookyeti.com/post/solo-leveling-webtoon-manhwa-series)
- Genshin / miHoYo: 3D form with 2D cel-shaded look, by deliberate art direction. — [GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1027538/-Genshin-Impact-Crafting-an)
- Arcane: realistic volume and lighting with hand-painted texture and 2D FX. — [RedShark News](https://redsharknews.com/why-netflixs-arcane-looks-so-good-how-fortiche-ramped-up-the-animation-pipeline)

### Inferences
- A shared pattern: pick one or two axes to make realistic (light, anatomy or texture) and keep the others stylized.

### Gaps
- I found no primary interviews in which Kuvshinov, Wit or MAPPA staff explicitly discuss their realism-versus-stylization choices.

## Common mistakes and best practices; keeping a character recognizable

### Takeaway
The main failure is a mismatch: realistic parts combined with stylized parts, or realistic looks combined with stylized motion. The main tool for consistency is a fixed design reference (model sheet or turnaround) plus a strong silhouette and shape language.

### Cited Findings
- Mismatch between 3D shape and material lowers affinity for a character. Realistic faces lose credibility when their animation doesn't fit their level of visual realism. Less realistic characters generally suppress the uncanny valley response. — [Eurographics EGVE 2023 paper](https://diglib7.eg.org/bitstream/handle/10.2312/egve20231314/063-071.pdf)
- Viewers reject a character when movement, expression and appearance don't match. Example: the first Sonic film design, where human-like eyes and teeth sat on a cartoon body. — [CORDIS](https://cordis.europa.eu/article/id/444372-what-makes-a-face-fall-into-the-uncanny-valley)
- The uncanny valley is debated as strict science; Mori framed it as design guidance. — [Unity glossary](https://unity.com/glossary/uncanny-valley)
- Features should match each other in level of stylization. — [Tutsplus](https://design.tutsplus.com/tutorials/how-to-draw-anime-heads-and-faces--cms-31884)
- Model sheets make the work of many artists look "as if one artist created the drawings". Standard turnaround views are front, 3/4 front, side, 3/4 back and back, with construction lines to keep proportions. — [Wikipedia: Model sheet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Model_sheet); [Clip Studio: Model sheets](https://www.clipstudio.net/how-to-draw/archives/164740)
- Shape language comes first and can come from the outfit as well as the body. A character should be recognizable as a solid black silhouette, and small signature props should stay legible at thumbnail size. — [BINUS: Designing characters with personality](https://binus.ac.id/bandung/dkv/2025/11/04/designing-characters-with-personality-from-sketch-to-screen); [Artfolio: turnaround sheets](https://www.artfolio.com/article/character-turnaround-sheets-impress-recruiters-with-silhouette-clarity)
- Over-realistic Shinkai-style characters start to read as CG renders (informal source). — [Anifusion](https://anifusion.ai/style/makoto-shinkai-style)

### Inferences
- Best practices for AI image generation in this repo: attach a character sheet image each time (the existing `image_refs` mechanism), keep a fixed text block for signature traits (hair shape and color, one accessory, outfit palette), and keep one stylization level per series, with no mixing of photoreal eyes or skin pores and anime eyes.
- Fixed signature traits (hairstyle silhouette, one prop, color palette) carry recognizability better than facial details, which AI tends to drift on.

### Gaps
- I found no sourced, systematic list of "common semi-realism mistakes" from a major tutorial site (Proko or ArtStation). The mistakes above come from uncanny-valley research and general tutorials.
- I found no source on keeping a character consistent specifically in AI-generated semi-realistic anime images.
